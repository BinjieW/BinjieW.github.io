CLIP experiments · 3,000 items

Two image-based scores.

We compare two six-view CLIP scores against existing Sonnet 5 YES/NO checklist labels. Both produce only limited separation between the two groups.

Contrastive Δ = meanᵥ[(sim(Mᵥ, positive) − sim(Mᵥ, negative)) − (sim(Oᵥ, positive) − sim(Oᵥ, negative))]

Asks whether each modified view moved toward the satisfied description relative to the original.

Directional CLIP = meanᵥ cosine(E(Mᵥ) − E(Oᵥ), E(target) − E(source))

Aligns the visual direction of change with the textual direction of the requested edit.

Case studies

Example score behavior.

The examples show cases where each CLIP score agrees or disagrees with the existing checklist label.

Contrastive agreement · global recoloring

Gold → red is visible in every view. Positive and negative captions differ primarily in color, producing a 98.8th-percentile YES score.

Contrastive disagreement · localized door

The wooden door occupies a small region inside a large scene rebuild. Whole-image semantics dominate, producing a high-confidence NO score.

Directional agreement · color edit

Object and framing remain stable while yellow becomes blue. All six image-difference vectors align with the text change.

Directional disagreement · preservation constraint

The artifact moves strongly toward “organic,” but loses its extrusion identity. Directional CLIP rewards change without enforcing the invariant.
