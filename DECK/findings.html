Things we noticed along the way.

Observations beyond the main tables: how noisy verification is, which judges are strict, why metrics mislead, why voting does not help, and what makes a task hard.

About the tasks and programs

Same size as 3DCodeBench, more structure

Our reconstructed programs are about as long as 3DCodeBench programs, but organized for editing: 28.6 vs 19.7 functions per program, 8.9 vs 5.8 reused helpers, call hierarchies 7.2 vs 3.9 levels deep, and 22.9 vs 15.0 named parameters. BlendNet scripts have none of this (0.1 functions on average).

Some tasks nobody solves, some everybody does

38 of 384 tasks are failed by all 14 models, and 12 are passed by all. Procedural 3DCodeBench objects are the hardest to edit (23.8% mean all-pass), ahead of furniture (31.8%), manufacturing (38.9%), and architecture (39.4%).

Crashes are not only about difficulty

Claude Sonnet 4.6 executes only 69% of its L1 edits but 97% at L6, and Claude Opus 4.8 drops to 64% at L2. Their syntax-valid rate is 84–86%: these failures come from broken output, not hard edits.

Even strong models misread geometry

Asked to narrow a sink basin from side to side, GPT-6-astra, GPT-5.6-sol, and Claude Sonnet 5 all shorten it front to back instead; Gemma 4 31B gets it right. See the edit-not-made cases →
